Measurements

Key idea: Two figures are congruent if and only if a sequence of rigid transformations (translations, reflections and rotations) maps one figure exactly onto the other.

Because rigid transformations never change lengths or angles, every pair of corresponding sides and corresponding angles of congruent figures are equal. If any side or angle doesn't match, no sequence of rigid motions can ever work.

    Questions about why rigid transformations and congruence go together.

    No grid? No problem. Without coordinates there are no rules like (x, y) → (−x, y) to use. Instead, you use what each transformation keeps the same, and two tools to copy it exactly.

    A compass copies distances: open it to a length, and it will draw that same length anywhere. A straightedge (a ruler used without its markings) draws lines. A reflection keeps each point the same distance from the mirror line. A translation moves every point the same length and direction as a given arrow. A rotation keeps each point the same distance from the center and turns it through a given angle.

    Red marks are the current step. Grey marks are earlier steps. A thick faded red bar shows a length the compass is measuring.
